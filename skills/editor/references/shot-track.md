# Shot track and transitions (learned on the MMH videos)

Style-specific numbers here (median shot length, transition counts) are the MMH defaults; the active style's `edit` section overrides them.

- Cut on phrases, one frame before the word: `cut(i) = wf(i) - 1`. Median shot 0.9 s (16:9) / 1.1 s (9:16); nothing over ~3.2 s except a deliberate final hold. The hook cuts fastest.
- **Give the viewer time to read each picture.** Never a run of shots under about 0.7 s each when every one is a new idea. Ryan v2 went canvas → T-Mobile (0.4 s) → phone, and the user couldn't process it. Prefer one continuous move: a drift across a canvas, or a push into a card until it fills the frame.
- **Stills that could be video:** prefer real footage of the person with the thing (Ryan putting the gin bottle in the golf ad, instead of a still inside a news report). If only a still exists, give it a Vox-style move: hold on the subject, then on the named word snap fast and settle smoothly onto the object (Ryan's face → the Aviation case).
- **Software UIs** (ChatGPT, LinkedIn, apps): rebuild the screen 1:1 in code for the right date. Put the camera on the input and type the real text in, word-synced, with key-press SFX. Never use a blurry screenshot of the UI out of an ad.
- **Minimal type-on plates are one line.** A second line ruins the style: move the rest of the sentence onto new footage (Ryan v2: "even without your name on it").
- Hook for ad stories: show the ad's most famous moment (e.g. the FEVICOL sign) for 2–3 s, then a pattern interrupt (freeze + RGB split, crop close, glitch lines) into the VO's question.
- Every line gets a literal, specific real picture. Abstract ideas become one concrete object. Facts and numbers become a **document with a highlighter wipe** (6 f, #F4EA3A, multiply). If the real document can't be obtained, show the subject on footage instead; a typeset summary page is allowed only for published numbers, clearly labelled, and the user is told.
- When the story revolves around one image (a famous photo or ad), use the real one and make it a recurring object: full frame, then a print with a white border on the plate, crops of it for every detail the script names (16:9 boxes in source pixels, slow push), then callbacks ("the names from that photo", "every version of that photo" as real published versions laid down as prints).
- **The hero image must stay up long enough to read:** about 4 s in the hook, with the print held ≥ 2 s. Full frame → print → full frame is one continuous move (the photo shrinks into the print showing the same crop, holds with a slow push, then the camera pushes back into it on the next cut). A hard flip to a small print and straight back reads as a glitch; the user flagged both the short hold and the flip.
- Moves inside one image (whip pan from Thiel to Levchin along the photo) use the same image with a 4-frame horizontal motion blur (SVG `feGaussianBlur stdDeviation="38 0"`), not a cut to another shot.
- **Showcasing Gridd's own reels** (a carousel, grid or cards for "this is what we do at Gridd"): the user judges them by what is on screen while each card faces the camera.
  - Compute the frame each card reaches the front, and make its clip play a self-explanatory moment then: a face plus the big word, or a headline with its highlight. Cold brew v2 used Anand Mahindra + "Mahindra.", the Maggi mother smiling into "Two minutes.", the Wakefit headline, and Ryan + his name.
  - Keep 16:9 reels in 16:9 cards. Cold brew v1 centre-cropped them into portrait cards, and the Maggi card showed only the mother's hand.
  - Cold brew v2 layout: 8 cards on a 45° ring (r 900, cards 820x461); 4 turn to the front in 7-frame eased moves and hold about 1 s each, with neighbours peeking at the edges. Each clip freezes on frame 0 until 4 frames before it arrives, then plays. A soft whoosh on each move.
- A breath every 5–15 s: a cool grey paper plate (#E5E3E7–#EDECEF, radial vignette, faint paper texture), with spread words, or with a rounded footage card (24 px radius, soft shadow) that can open to full frame in 8–15 f. For digital subjects use the bible's plate variants (a phone mock-up on a grid, fanned cards, a 2×2 grid, a rounded screen card).
- Film or TV lines that MMH plays as clips (Goodfellas, Scarface) become **quote beats**: a 3–4 s VO gap over stills from that actual film (FILMGRAB) with the period treatment MMH uses (VHS OSD `PLAY ▶ / SP / timecode` + scanlines + RGB split for Goodfellas; zoom-blur in and a white muzzle-flash frame out for Scarface). The line is set in white caps with glow, words jitter in at odd sizes for 2–3 f then settle; the punch word ("GANGSTER") assembles from flying letters over ~10 f. Our voice doesn't speak them.
- Pop-culture references in a line ("straight out of The Sopranos", "thinks Thiel is the Godfather") get the real show/film still with CRT + RGB split, the real logotype where MMH used it, and a glitch-block cut to the real person.
- Comparisons: a hard 50/50 split with plain labels at caption size, or a 2×2 grid.
- Transitions: hard cuts by default (75–90%). Only 3–5 punctuation moments per 35 s: white flash 1–2 f, zoom punch 4 f (1.18 → 1 with blur), a blur-in from white for the hook, blur-bloom 5 f, card→full, a 4 f whip (scale 2 → 1, heavy horizontal blur, direction matching the next shot's motion), a 3 f Bezier slide with a whoosh, crop close/open (`bible-transitions.md`). No whips on every cut. Use Eyecandy to agree anything unusual first.
- End: the last line's picture as a callback (often the opening image), hold ~0.9–1.2 s, then the brand card. BerriBot: the real lockup, teal, 2.4 s, no spoken CTA. Gridd AI: the mark redrawn in SVG on cream and assembled block by block, name typed on, no captions, the CTA voiced over it.
